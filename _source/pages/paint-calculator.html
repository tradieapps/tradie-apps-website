How the paint calculation works

Wall area is the room's perimeter times the wall height: 2 × (length + width) × height. Doors and windows are taken off, then the area is multiplied by the number of coats and divided by the paint's coverage.

Most interior acrylics cover roughly 12–16 m² per litre per coat, or around 350–400 sq ft per US gallon, on smooth, sealed walls. Fresh plaster, bare timber and rough or dark surfaces take more, so use the figure on your tin and allow a primer or sealer coat where needed.

Example

A 4 m × 3.5 m bedroom with 2.4 m walls, one door and one window: 2 × 7.5 × 2.4 = 36 m², less 3.4 m² of openings = 32.6 m². Two coats at 12 m²/L is about 5.4 L, so one 4 L tin plus a 2 L tin, or a 10 L tin if you're doing more than one room.

Tips

  • Buy enough of one batch to finish each colour, so the shade matches.
  • Ceilings usually take a dedicated ceiling paint, so work them out separately.
  • Feature walls: work out the feature colour as its own wall (width × height).
